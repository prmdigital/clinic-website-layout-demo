small teeth with a very big role

Primary teeth (also called baby or milk teeth) are vital. They help direct the growth of the jaw, hold space for the adult teeth developing underneath, and help with chewing and a confident smile.

Children's teeth go through many changes and are very transitional, which is why they need regular monitoring by someone who specializes in them.

what makes a pediatric dentist different

Pediatric dentists complete a further three years of training to become experts in:

Children's teeth

How children's teeth differ from adult teeth, and the special role they play.

Growth & development

Monitoring how the teeth and jaws grow, so problems are picked up early.

Emotional development

Understanding how children think and feel, to make every visit a positive one.

Restoring teeth

The best ways to restore and protect young teeth when treatment is needed.

five ways we protect little teeth

Brush, floss & repeat

A complete home routine is the front line against cavities and gingivitis. Dr. Your Name recommends brushing twice a day, for at least two minutes each time.

Fluoride varnish

The Canadian and American Academies of Pediatric Dentistry recommend fluoride varnish twice a year to greatly reduce the chance of tooth decay.

Sealants

Molars have pits and fissures that can trap food and plaque. Sealing them with a sealant helps prevent decay in these hard-to-clean areas.

Mouth guards

Mouth guards help prevent injuries to teeth during sports and recreational fun, like riding a bike.

Healthy eating

Good oral hygiene habits and a good diet go hand in hand in preventing cavities. Snack ideas →

Regular check-ups

Regular visits let us monitor your child's dental health and stop small problems from becoming big ones.

prevention questions

How often should my child brush?

Twice a day, for at least two minutes each time. Encourage your child to brush on their own, then finish the job for them.

What is fluoride varnish?

A protective coating painted onto the teeth. The Canadian and American Academies of Pediatric Dentistry recommend it twice a year to greatly reduce the chance of tooth decay.

What are sealants?

Back molar teeth have lots of pits and fissures where food and plaque get trapped. A sealant covers these grooves to help prevent decay.

Does my child need a mouth guard?

If your child plays sports or enjoys active fun like biking, a mouth guard can help prevent injuries to their teeth. Ask Dr. Your Name at your next visit.

brushing & diet questions

what is interceptive orthodontics?

Children's teeth are transitional and go through many changes. Orthodontic needs can often be picked up at this early stage. Treating them now is called interceptive orthodontics, also known as First-Phase or Phase-One orthodontics.

It takes advantage of your child's natural growth potential and can lessen orthodontic treatment time later. Early treatment typically begins around age 8 or 9.

Dr. Your Name works with a group of orthodontists to best suit your family's needs and convenience.

Making room for adult teeth while the jaw is still growing.

Front teeth that stick out are more prone to injury. Early care can help.

orthodontic questions

What age is best for early orthodontics?

Children go through enormous growth between ages 6 and 13. Orthodontic needs can often be spotted in this window, and early (Phase-One) treatment typically begins around age 8 or 9.

Does early treatment mean my child won't need braces later?

Interceptive treatment takes advantage of your child's growth and can lessen orthodontic treatment time later. Dr. Your Name will talk you through what to expect for your child.

a complete blueprint of your child's mouth

A dental X-ray gives Dr. Your Name a complete picture of your child's mouth. It helps her:

  • Supervise teeth as they come in
  • Monitor for developmental anomalies in growth
  • Detect problems that can't be seen in a visual exam

Like any problem, the earlier we find cavities, bone diseases or tooth injuries, the sooner we can begin preventing or treating them.

x-ray safety

X-ray images are an important tool for any dentist. They help us pick up dental decay early and see how far decay has spread, and they help us notice developmental anomalies in growth.

At our office we use digital X-ray machines, which give off up to seventy percent less radiation than conventional X-rays.

x-ray questions

Are dental X-rays safe for children?

Safety is our main concern. We use digital X-ray machines, which give off up to 70% less radiation than conventional X-rays.

Why does my child need X-rays?

X-rays show what can't be seen with the naked eye: early cavities, how far decay has spread, tooth injuries, and how adult teeth are developing.

We make every effort to keep our young patients comfortable and safe. For children with dental anxiety, sedation can help, from routine cleanings to fillings and other treatments.

one size does not fit all

As specialists in pediatric dentistry, we're trained to help most children feel at ease using recognized behaviour management techniques.

Some children have more severe anxiety, or are simply at the pre-cooperative age. For these patients we offer different levels of sedation.

Dr. Your Name will explain how each option works, so you can decide together what's best for your child.

levels of sedation we offer

1

Nitrous oxide ("laughing gas")

Also known as gas and air, the same gas many mothers use during labour. It reduces anxiety and has a mild pain-relieving effect.

2

Oral sedation with nitrous oxide

Medicines that reduce anxiety and cause some drowsiness. Your child is relaxed but not fully asleep. This can be done here at our office.

3

General anaesthesia

For especially nervous or pre-cooperative children, general anaesthesia may be needed. Dr. Your Name will discuss this with you and answer any questions about what's most suitable for your child.

?

Have questions?

Every child is different. Please ask Dr. Your Name for more details. We're happy to talk through each option, how it works, and what to expect before, during and after.

sedation questions

Is my child asleep with oral sedation?

No. Oral sedation reduces anxiety and causes some drowsiness, but your child is not fully asleep.

What does laughing gas do?

Nitrous oxide reduces anxiety and has a mild pain-relieving (analgesic) effect.

How do I know which option is right?

Dr. Your Name will explain how each option works and answer your questions, so you can decide together what's best for your child.

when is it an emergency?

As children grow up and lead more active lives, the chance of injury goes up. Blows to the head or mouth can seriously damage teeth and need immediate attention from a children's dentist.

Some emergencies are easy to spot. For example, a lot of bleeding from the mouth after a blow to the face is a clear sign to seek emergency dental care right away.

Others are less obvious. If your child has ongoing jaw pain with no clear cause, it could point to a problem with their teeth. Call us and ask for an emergency appointment with Dr. Your Name.

Knocked-out tooth

Call us right away. Never put a baby tooth back in. Keep an adult tooth in cold milk if you can't put it back.

Bleeding after a fall

Heavy bleeding from the mouth after a blow to the face needs emergency dental care right away.

Ongoing jaw pain

Jaw pain with no clear cause could be a dental problem. Call to request an emergency appointment.

!

Serious injury or after hours?

If your child has a head injury, trouble breathing, or bleeding that won't stop, call 911 or go to the nearest hospital emergency department.

privacy policy

Your Clinic ("we", "our" or "us") is committed to protecting your privacy.

✦

This Privacy Policy explains how we collect, use and safeguard your personal information when you visit our website at yourclinic.com or use our services.

Information we collect

  • Parent or guardian's name, email and phone number
  • Child's name and age
  • Appointment and contact preferences
  • CDCP (Canadian Dental Care Plan) usage
  • Information sent to us by referring dentists or doctors through our referral form
  • IP address, browser details and website usage (through cookies or analytics tools)

How we use your information

We use the information we collect to:

  • Book and manage dental appointments
  • Communicate with you about our services or your eligibility
  • Confirm CDCP coverage where applicable
  • Improve our website's functionality and marketing performance

We do not sell or share your personal data with third parties for profit.

Sharing your information

We may share your information with trusted service providers, such as:

  • Online booking systems
  • Analytics platforms (for example Google Analytics and Meta Pixel)
  • Government programs (to verify CDCP coverage)

Use of cookies

We use cookies and similar tracking tools to analyze traffic and deliver relevant ads. You can opt out of or manage cookies in your browser settings.

Your rights

You can:

  • Access or correct your personal data
  • Withdraw your consent at any time
  • Ask us to delete your data

Children's privacy

We only collect information about children through their parent or guardian (or a referring health professional). Our website and services are intended for use by adults on behalf of children under 18.

Data protection

We use secure methods to protect your information from unauthorized access, loss or misuse.
